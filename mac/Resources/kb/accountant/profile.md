# Accounts payable clerk: coding and posting supplier invoices

## Role
- Sabine, 57, has run accounts payable at a machine builder near Stuttgart for 24 years. She retires in 18 months.
- Specialty: knowing which supplier invoice needs a different cost center, a hold or a second approval, none of which is written in the 2019 process document.
- Her new colleague Lena, 26, started on Monday and learns by watching Sabine's screen.

## The moment
- Thursday, 4:10 pm, two days before month-end close, 60 invoices still open.
- Sabine works fast and mostly silent: moves one invoice to another cost center, holds a second, sends a third for a second approval.

## Job duties
- Check each supplier invoice against the order, the supplier and the amount.
- Code it to the right cost center, then Post it, Hold it, or Send for approval.
- Protect the books at close: capex versus opex, duplicates, approvals.

## Tools on screen (sandbox ERP, fake data)
- Open invoices list (60 open). Invoice detail page with: Supplier, Company code, Invoice date, Description, Category, Amount, Cost center, Asset no.
- Cost center values: 4711 is opex, 0400 is capex.
- Buttons: Hold, Send for approval, Post.
- Sandbox invoices: 4471 equipment over the capex line, 4468 from the supplier that double-bills in December, 4473 from the Czech subsidiary. Supplier names and the amounts other than €7,200 are sandbox inventions.

## Rules in her head (from the challenge brief)
- Equipment over €5,000 is always capex: re-code from opex (4711) to capex (0400).
- No asset number, no capex booking.
- Unknown supplier: stop and ask the controller.
- One supplier double-bills every December: hold its invoice until it is released.
- Invoices from the Czech subsidiary need a second approval.

## What is not written anywhere
- Whether the December hold applies to every supplier or just one, and who decides when to release it. The debrief must ask.
- Which other suppliers or entities get special handling. Ask: "what would you never do?"
- The reason behind each rule in her own words. The Work Map stores the quote.

## Typical day
- Morning: match new invoices to orders, clear the clean ones.
- Before close: chase approvals, hold suspicious ones, make sure capex has asset numbers.
- Close week: the pile is large and every judgment call has a deadline.

## What novices get wrong
- Reach for the default opex code (4711) because the system pre-fills it, even for a €7,200 equipment invoice.
- Post a capex booking without an asset number.
- Post a known double-biller in December because the invoice looks perfectly normal.
- Post an invoice from the Czech subsidiary alone instead of sending it for approval.
- Guess when a supplier is unknown instead of stopping to ask the controller.

## How the buddy should behave
- Learn mode: stay quiet while she types and reads. At a pause ask one short question about something visible: "You moved that one to capex. What made you do that?"
- Do not ask what the screen already answers (amount, supplier, date). Ask for the reason and the limit.
- Teach mode: when the new hire reaches for Post with the wrong code, stop them before the click: "Sabine would stop here. Why do you think?" Then replay Sabine's reason in her words.
- Debrief questions to keep for later: December hold scope, who releases it, what else triggers a second approval.
- Personal data on screen (names, bank details) is redacted in transcripts; anything Sabine marks off the record is not stored.
