# Support agent: customer support deciding refunds and exceptions

## Role
- Works the billing queue of a subscription software company (sandbox: a fictional helpdesk with fake customers).
- Specialty: deciding refund and exception requests that the written policy does not fully cover.
- Experienced agents know the unwritten context: open incidents, disputes, account tiers, who must approve what.

## Job duties
- Read the ticket, the customer message and the order history.
- Check the payment, the refund window and the customer's account.
- Decide: Refund, Escalate, or reply without refunding. Leave a note so the next agent knows why.

## Tools on screen
- Ticket view: customer message, order history table, customer card (name, Account tier, region, account manager).
- Banner for incidents ("Open incident INC-2043: Service outage, EU region").
- Payment and refund panel: Original payment (card ending), Payment status, Policy line (Refund window: open or closed), Refund amount.
- Buttons: Refund, Escalate.

## Example policy values (labelled examples for the demo, not a real company policy)
- Refund window: 30 days after the order.
- Agent refund limit: 250 EUR. Above it a second approver must sign off.

## Rules in the expert's head
- Outside the 30-day window is normally a no, unless an incident hit this customer. An open outage is on us.
- Never refund to a different card or account than the original payment.
- A chargeback is already filed: do not refund. It would pay the customer twice.
- Enterprise or VIP account: loop in the account manager before any refund or exception.
- Refund over the agent limit needs a second approver.
- "The customer threatens a public review" is not a reason by itself.

## What is not written anywhere
- Which incidents count as "hit this customer" and for how long after an incident the exception applies.
- When a goodwill refund is acceptable and who can grant it.
- How to answer an angry customer while still saying no.

## Typical day
- Morning: the queue is full of easy refunds inside the window. Clear them fast.
- Midday: the hard tickets: disputes, enterprise customers, requests that look odd.
- Afternoon: incidents. When there is an outage, refund requests spike and the normal window rules bend.

## What novices get wrong
- Treat the policy as the whole truth and refund on the first angry message.
- Give way to pressure: public review, social media, "I will cancel today".
- Refund to the card the customer names now, not the one that paid.
- Refund an order that is already in dispute with the bank.
- Handle an enterprise customer like any other and skip the account manager.
- Refund above their limit because the amount "looks small for this customer".

## How the buddy should behave
- Learn mode: stay quiet while the agent reads the order history and types the reply. At a pause ask one short question about something visible on screen: "The window has closed, yet you refund. What changes the answer for this customer?"
- Do not ask what the ticket already says. Ask for the reason, the limit, the moment to escalate.
- Teach mode: before the click on Refund, say what the expert looked at, in the expert's words, and ask the novice what they expect to happen next.
- Personal data (names, card endings) is redacted in transcripts; anything the expert takes off the record is not stored.
